STR VS. MTR: WHICH RENTAL STRATEGY FITS YOUR MARKET

September 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Short-term rentals (STR) and mid-term rentals (MTR) both get pitched as the "better" cash-flow strategy, but the truth is they solve different problems in different markets. Picking the wrong one for your specific property is how investors end up with high vacancy and a strategy that fights their location instead of using it.

What Actually Separates STR From MTR

STR means nightly or weekly stays, marketed on platforms like Airbnb and VRBO, competing directly with hotels. MTR means furnished stays typically 30-90+ days, serving traveling professionals, insurance relocations, and traveling nurses — markets that never touch a hotel-comparison site at all.

Where STR Wins

Tourist and event-driven markets with strong seasonal demand and, critically, STR-friendly local regulation. STR revenue can be substantially higher per night than MTR, but it comes with real management overhead — turnover cleaning, guest communication, and dynamic pricing — and real vacancy risk in the off-season.

Where MTR Wins

Markets near hospitals, corporate relocation hubs, and universities, where demand is steady year-round rather than seasonal. MTR carries lower nightly revenue than a strong STR night, but dramatically lower turnover, lower cleaning cost, and far more predictable occupancy — which is often the better trade for an investor who isn't running this as a full-time operation.

Regulation Decides Before Revenue Does

A growing number of cities restrict or outright ban short-term rentals, or require permits that cap how many STR units can exist in a given area. Check local ordinances before you model STR revenue on a property — a market can look perfect on paper and be legally closed to the strategy entirely. MTR faces far fewer of these restrictions since it's typically treated the same as a standard furnished lease.

Run Both Scenarios Before You Commit

The right answer is specific to the property and the market, not a universal rule. Before locking in a strategy, model realistic occupancy and revenue for both STR and MTR against the same property — not the best-case number from an Airbnb calculator, but a number that accounts for your actual local demand and regulatory environment.
