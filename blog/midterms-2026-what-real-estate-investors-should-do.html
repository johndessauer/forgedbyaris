AFTER THE 2026 MIDTERMS: WHAT REAL ESTATE INVESTORS SHOULD DO NOW

November 5, 2026 · 4 min read

Election week is loud. Headlines move fast, opinions move faster, and it's easy to feel like you should do something right now.

Here's the good news: for real estate investors, the most useful thing an election gives you is clarity. The map is mostly drawn. The guessing game is mostly over. And the fundamentals that actually drive real estate deals — local supply, local demand, financing costs, and your own math — haven't changed overnight.

Let's walk through what we know, what to watch, and how to position.

Where things stand (as of Thursday, Nov 5)

Major news outlets, including AP, CBS News, Reuters, and The Guardian, project that control of both chambers of Congress will change hands when the new Congress is seated in January. That means the next two years will feature a divided federal government: the White House and Congress led by different parties.

A few races, including Senate contests in Alaska and Mississippi and a number of House seats, were still uncalled as of this writing. Treat early headlines as directional, not final. Seat counts will keep shifting for days.

Sources: AP News, CBS News, Reuters, The Guardian, Ballotpedia news summaries, Nov 4–5, 2026. These are news summaries, not official vote counts.

Why divided government is often workable for investors

We're not here to cheer for anyone. We're here to underwrite. And from an underwriting standpoint, divided government tends to mean:

  • Fewer sweeping federal changes. Big swings in law are harder to pass when power is split. That makes the rules you're investing under more predictable for the next two years.
  • Uncertainty settles into a known map. Before an election, every possible outcome is on the table. Afterward, you can plan around a narrower range of scenarios.
  • Local levers matter even more. Zoning, permitting, property taxes, and landlord-tenant rules are set mostly by states, counties, and cities. Several state legislative chambers also changed control this cycle, so watch your own statehouse closely.

Clarity is an investor's friend. Disciplined investors usually do their best work when the noise peaks and everyone else is waiting on the sidelines.

The number that matters more than any headline: your rate

Interest rates hit your real estate deals far more directly than campaign news. Here's the current picture:

  • Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the 30-year fixed mortgage at 7.18% as of Nov 5, 2026, down from 7.24% the week before and marking the third straight weekly decline. A year earlier it was 6.22%.
  • Daily rate-lock data showed the 30-year near 7.17% on Nov 3.

Rates have drifted lower for a few weeks, but they're still well above a year ago. The Federal Reserve, inflation data, and the bond market will steer them, not election night. Build your numbers on today's rate, and treat any drop as upside.

Policy watch list (no predictions, just categories)

Keep an eye on these over the next few months, without assuming any particular outcome:

  1. Interest-rate path. Fed decisions and the 10-year Treasury yield.
  2. Housing supply. Any federal or state efforts on building, permitting, or zoning reform.
  3. Tax treatment of investing. Depreciation, 1031 exchanges, capital gains, and expiring provisions. If anything changes, it usually takes time, so plan with your CPA rather than reacting to headlines.
  4. Local rules. Property tax assessments, rental regulations, and short-term rental ordinances in your target market.

The FORGE playbook for this week

Here's how to respond, and none of it depends on who won what:

  1. Underwrite conservatively. Use today's rate, realistic repair costs, and a cushion for vacancy and holding time.
  2. Stick to your max offer math. Know your ARV, your costs, and your maximum allowable offer before you call. If the numbers don't work, the real estate deal doesn't work, whatever the news says.
  3. Keep two exits. Every property should work at least two ways, for example as a flip and as a rental, or as a wholesale assignment and as a hold.
  4. Watch local data, not national noise. Track inventory, days on market, and price cuts in your zip codes each week. That's where real estate deals show up first.
  5. Work your lists and follow up. Motivated sellers don't stop being motivated because of an election. Pre-foreclosure, absentee, high-equity, vacant, and tax-delinquent lists still produce. Consistent follow-up beats perfect timing.

The bottom line

Elections come and go. Good real estate deals are found the same way they always have been: with steady math, honest underwriting, and the discipline to keep working while others wait for the dust to settle.

The dust is settling. That makes this a good week to get to work.
